# Run Bun! — Guard artwork generation and editing protocol

Read [shared artwork rules](../../../GEN.md), [Bun design](../Bun/GEN.md), and [Dog design](../Dog/GEN.md). Current user directions take precedence.

## Purpose

Guard depicts Buns rallying to support a Bun threatened by a pursuing Dog. One to three or four helpers use cunning, skills, resourcefulness, and their surroundings. An occasional strong Bun is appropriate, but brute strength is not the defining theme. The threatened Bun begins rallying too.

Show the intervention in progress. Neither escape nor defeat is established. The Dog remains a credible threat; do not portray a completed rescue or victory. Keep physical actions, grips, hinges, ropes, wheels, and escape routes coherent.

## Style and production

### Corrections from the first rejected batch

Cooperation does not mean clustering everyone together. Distribute helpers around the environment with distinct complementary jobs, elevations, cover, and approach directions. Some scenes may group Buns, but do not repeat a lineup of adjacent Buns pushing one object. Their separate actions must visibly combine against the Dog; use environment creatively and preserve readable causal connections.

Every Bun needs unmistakable established Victorian steampunk costuming: distinct hats or headgear, brass goggles, leather belts and pouches, tailored layered clothing, and purposeful accessories. Do not reduce the cast to plain-coated rabbits. Trace each arm from shoulder to hand: exactly two arms and two hands per Bun, no extra reaching hands, fused limbs, or unexplained grips. Review gate hinge attachment, swing direction, force direction, and each character's side of the gate. The rejected Wrong Turn rebuild had faulty geometry and an extra arm; do not reuse it as a reference.

Vary everything across the batch: Dog identity, Bun cast, fur, clothing, poses, expressions, viewpoint, composition, location, architecture, terrain, weather, time of day, and sky details. Do not copy a reference scene's skyline, blimp, glass dome, mountains, or costume ensemble. Use references for character identity and rendering only. Compare the four scenes together for repeated motifs before acceptance.

Use a different existing Dog from the installed Dog roster as a direct visual input for each scene, or a distinctly varied design faithful to that roster. Do not reuse the Dog from a generic action-scene reference. The first four drafts repeated the same unsuitable Dog and are rejected.

The Dog must already be in immediate peril from the intervention: committed momentum, a closing obstacle, or a taut snag engaging its path. A distant Dog with ample room to sidestep a setup does not create a contest. Show active resistance and uncertainty, not a completed defeat. Preserve the Dog's menace and the Buns' vulnerability simultaneously.

Buns should look worried, angry, or determined. An occasional mischievous expression is acceptable. No cheerful smiles or joyous rescue atmosphere. Their friend rallies under pressure; nobody celebrates a result that has not happened.

Check these three requirements explicitly before accepting any future Guard image: distinct roster-faithful Dog, immediate credible peril to the Dog, and emotionally appropriate Buns.

Use inspected approved installed artwork as direct visual references. Preserve established adorable Bun proportions, short rabbit legs, rounded paws, clothing coverage, and distinct natural fur and outfits. Dogs are huge, powerful, menacing canines with two eyes and four limbs. Use richly colored detailed Victorian steampunk settings, sharp foreground and background, no blur, and no baked-in frames or text.

Generate four separate images at a time, not a contact sheet. Save named PNGs in this Guard folder, retaining generated originals and revisions under Resize. Use the existing Frame Guard.png as layout reference; keep important faces and actions clear of its side ornaments. Preserve existing files. CSV and rendering integration remain deferred; this guide does not authorize code changes.

## Approved twenty-scene roster

1. Wrong Turn — Helpers swing a garden gate across the Dog's path while another helps the threatened Bun toward a narrow gap; the gate is still moving.
2. Loose Ends — Helpers on opposite sides of an alley pull a fallen clothesline taut as their pursued friend ducks beneath it.
3. Market Forces — Helpers release and steer a produce cart across the Dog's route; the threatened Bun joins the push.
4. Unpleasant Opening — Helpers open patched umbrellas into a shaky barrier while their friend struggles to open a third.
5. Under New Management — Helpers emerge from burrows, clattering saucepan lids to divide the Dog's attention; their friend joins with a kettle.
6. Emergency Exit — Helpers lift a heavy street grate and reach from below while the threatened Bun helps lift; another distracts with a coat behind a bollard.
7. A Clean Getaway — Helpers tip a laundry trough across the courtyard while their friend reaches for a hanging sheet; the Dog approaches the spreading suds.
8. Load-Bearing Friendship — Helpers brace a plank across a narrow ditch while a third grips its retrieval rope; their friend is still crossing. Keep the original ditch, not a chasm.
9. Special Delivery — Helpers maneuver an open delivery crate containing their threatened friend beneath a low fence while another distracts with its lid. Title collision with existing Lure card must be resolved before CSV registration.
10. Curtain Call — Helpers lower a heavy market awning across the pursuit; the threatened Bun helps pull its edge.
11. Loose Slates — Helpers on a low potting shed shift loose roof slates toward the edge while their threatened friend braces a rake across the passage below. No impact or injury shown.
12. Dock and Cover — Helpers pull a rowboat alongside a landing and extend an oar to their threatened friend; the boat is still out of reach.
13. Going Up — Helpers crank a cargo-basket hoist while their threatened friend reaches toward another helper; the Dog remains within the unresolved scene.
14. Table Manners — Helpers overturn a picnic table into a barricade; their friend joins the effort.
15. False Advertising — A helper operates a rabbit-shaped decoy bundle on a pulley while others guide the threatened Bun toward a side door; diversion not yet resolved.
16. Rolling Resistance — Helpers roll an empty barrel into a narrow passage and begin wedging it while their friend turns to help.
17. Occupational Hazard — A helper scatters wooden spools across a workshop floor while another reaches to help their friend onto a bench; the Dog approaches.
18. Hedge Your Bets — Helpers open a concealed hedge panel and reach for their friend while the Dog approaches; escape not completed.
19. Strength in Small Numbers — Helpers use a timber lever to move a heavy obstruction; their threatened friend joins them on the lever.
20. Local Knowledge — A helper on a signpost directs companions around a courtyard to open a narrow route through baskets for their threatened friend.

## Verification

Check anatomy, mechanism geometry, scene readability, distinct compositions, and unresolved outcomes before accepting each image. Report saved-file checks separately from Studio rendering. No Studio rendering has been verified for this batch.
