### Bun

Bun cards are the characters controlled by Bun players. Each player starts with two Buns, called a Brace. They begin face down; their owner may look at them. A Bun can escape, be injured, or be eaten. Escaped Buns provide Burrow plays. The same escaped or injured Bun returns at the start of the next interval; an eaten Bun is replaced. Cunning contributes to Guard, while the Chase modifier affects escape rolls.

### Nice

Nice cards help another player's Bun. During the other-player response window, each other Bun player may play one Nice card for each Bun being chased, or pass. Follow the card's targeting and effect text.

### Nasty

Nasty cards make trouble for another player's Bun. During the other-player response window, each other Bun player may play one Nasty card for each Bun being chased, or pass. This option is independent of Nice.

### Panic

Panic is a reaction to Startle. Only the chased Bun's owner may play it, and only for that chased Bun. The existing allowance is one Panic. Without Startle, there is no Panic opportunity; Guard failure and Chase rerolls do not reopen it.

### Prep

Each Bun player may place one Prep face down per Dog turn during the preparation window. Keep it in its owner's area without assigning a Bun in advance; there is no limit to the number kept face down. Activation is optional, and the card's named effect defines its timing. Choose the target when activating it and discard it after resolution. During a Chase, only the chased Bun's owner may flip Prep, and only for that chased Bun. The owner may flip any number of applicable Prep. Other players cannot activate Prep in response to that Chase. See Effects and Take a Dog Turn for the full response sequence.

### Scavenge

Scavenge is a Survival card type played during Preparation. Each Roaming Bun provides one Scavenge opportunity per Dog turn and uses its own Scavenge value for its check. Two Roaming Buns provide two independent opportunities; one provides one; none provides none. Burrowed, injured, and eaten Buns do not contribute opportunities or values. Do not average Buns' values or transfer one Bun's opportunity to another.

A Scavenge card requires a successful **Scavenge Check** before its effect can be carried out. Roll a d20 and add the acting Bun's Scavenge value and the Scavenge card's modifier. A modified total of **16 or higher** succeeds. A **natural 20 always succeeds** and a **natural 1 always fails**, regardless of modifiers.

Only Bun cards have a Scavenge value. Initial values are randomly assigned integers from **1 to 10**. Scavenge cards specify their own positive or negative check modifiers; powerful effects can carry negative modifiers.

Scavenge concerns finding and exchanging supplies, not Chase-roll bonuses.
Effects may involve your hand, the Survival deck, the shared Survival discard pile, or your own face-down Prep, as the card specifies. Discarded Prep can come from any player. Other players' hands remain off-limits.
Specific card effects, card count, and what happens to a Scavenge card after a failed check remain to be decided; the cards await implementation.


### Guard

Guard is played directly from hand during the Guard step, after the chased owner's Prep and before Chase rolls. All players make secret commitments for their eligible Buns, then reveal together; there is no clockwise choice order.

Each Guard card commits **one Roaming Bun you control** to protect **one chased Bun**. Choose the guarding Bun and the Bun it will protect.
You may play up to one Guard card per Roaming Bun you control, using a different guarding Bun for each card. You must have a Guard card for each Bun you commit. Burrowed, injured, and eaten Buns cannot be committed as guards. The helper count is not rolled and requires no printed Guard value.

Each Bun has one reusable choice card, separate from the Survival deck.
Guard and Pass are printed at opposite ends of the same face. Secretly orient the chosen end toward the Dog and place the card face down beside that Bun. Every eligible Bun must commit to Guard or Pass before any choice is revealed. You may choose Guard only if you have a separate Guard card in hand for each Bun you commit.

Once all choices are placed, they are locked. Reveal together by flipping each choice card sideways, keeping the same end toward the Dog. Do not flip end over end or rotate it on the table. Sideways arrows on the back show the flip direction; the back must look identical when rotated 180 degrees, with no top or bottom marker that could reveal the choice.
Play one Guard card from hand for each Guard choice; Pass spends none.
Choices cannot be changed after reveal. Keep the choice cards for reuse.

**Nasty prevents Guard:** If you played a Nasty card against a Bun during this Dog turn, you cannot play a Guard card to protect that Bun for the rest of the turn. This restriction applies to that player and that Bun, including any additional Chases during the same turn.

The chased Bun's owner rolls the shared defense d20 and adds the participating Buns' Cunning values, against the Dog's d20 plus Cunning. Success cancels the Chase without killing the Dog. Failure exposes all participating Buns, including helpers, to the Chase without reopening earlier response steps. Detailed ties, combining defended groups, and eligibility of already-chased helpers remain open; see Take a Dog Turn and Still to Decide.


### Dog

The Dog character is controlled by the Dog player. Draw one Dog at setup and place it face up. Its traits can be referenced by card effects, and its Cunning is used in Guard contests. The Dog uses Havoc cards to pursue Buns. When it catches a Bun, it chooses whether to eat or injure it under the Chase rules.

### Chase

A Chase card starts a pursuit against one eligible Bun and specifies any card-specific effects. The default limit is one Chase per Dog turn; a card may grant more. After declaring Chase and targets, resolve Startle, its Panic response, other players' Nice and Nasty, the chased owner's Prep, and Guard before Chase rolls. A Chase canceled by an effect stops without Chase rolls. The existing limit is one Chase against a Bun per Dog turn, with Divert and Guard interactions still open.

Some cards allow the Dog to make a second or additional Chase during the same turn. **Finish the current Chase completely before starting the next.**
Treat each additional Chase as a brand-new Chase, following the same rules and full sequence: choose an eligible target, optionally play Startle, resolve its Panic response, Nice and Nasty, the chased owner's Prep, Guard, Chase rolls, ties, outcomes, and scoring. Each Chase has its own Startle and response opportunities. Additional Chases do not restart Refresh, Prowl, or Preparation, and do not bypass the existing limit of one Chase against a Bun per Dog turn. Proceed to Cleanup after all Chases are finished. Additional Chases replace simultaneous multi-target Chase cards; helpers exposed by a failed Guard are still handled under Guard.

### Lure

Lure is a Havoc card played in the Dog's Prowl-and-Lure window before Bun preparation. It can bait Buns or affect subsequent Prep and Scavenge plays as its text specifies. Its play limit and individual effects remain to be defined.

### Prowl

Prowl is a Havoc card that reveals Buns without catching them. The Dog may play Prowl in the Prowl-and-Lure window, and it resolves immediately. Catching still requires a Chase. Cozy checks whether the Dog has used Prowl on that particular Bun; its Prowl lookback period remains undecided.

### Startle

The Dog may play one Startle after declaring the Chase and its targets. Startle opens a Panic response only for the chased Bun's owner, for that Bun. Startle is a Havoc card; individual effects remain to be defined.

## Traits

Traits are named attributes on Bun and Dog cards. Card effects can use them to identify eligible targets or specify bonuses, penalties, or other conditions. A trait does not grant an effect by itself; follow the text of the card that refers to it.

### Dog traits

The current Dog traits are:

- **Cruel** — Lets you finish your little speech. It likes the hopeful bit.
- **Cunning** — The emergency exit was its idea. So was the arrow pointing toward it.
- **Merciless** — Accepts apologies. Does not accept them as payment.
- **Patient** — Your hiding place has an excellent view of you eventually needing a piss.
- **Ravenous** — Calls it a food chain because calling it a queue upsets the Buns.
- **Reckless** — The fence says KEEP OUT. It reads that as a serving suggestion.
- **Relentless** — Still chasing the Bun whose missing poster has faded.

Each is a label that card effects may refer to, rather than a separate ability. The **Cunning** trait is distinct from the numeric Cunning stat.

A Dog can have more than one trait. For example, Gallows is **Ravenous** and **Relentless**. Just Wide Enough gives +3 to an escape roll against a **Reckless** Dog, so Gallows does not qualify based on its printed traits.

### Bun traits

Bun traits serve the same purpose: card effects can refer to them to identify eligible Buns or specify conditions. The defined Bun traits are:

- **Brave** — Stands up to monsters. The memorial portrait captures this beautifully.
- **Clever** — Knows seventeen ways out. Explaining them has used up sixteen.
- **Clumsy** — Trips over nothing. Nothing has an impressive body count.
- **Curious** — Found out what the screaming was about. Now contributing.
- **Empathic** — Understands the Dog is hungry. Wishes empathy came with a fucking off switch.
- **Foodie** — Can identify every herb in the marinade. This is becoming uncomfortably relevant.
- **Loyal** — Would never leave a friend behind. This makes the catering wonderfully predictable.
- **Nimble** — Can turn on a sixpence. The Dog is hoping for change.
- **Observant** — Spotted the trap, the teeth, and the suspicious absence of previous winners.
- **Resourceful** — Built an escape ladder from scraps. Nobody asks whose belt that was.
- **Selfish** — Believes every Bun deserves a chance. Preferably after theirs.
- **Sneaky** — Leaves no footprints, no witnesses, and a friend holding the blame.

These traits are defined but are not yet assigned to individual Buns.

## Cunning value

Cunning is the agreed name for a comparative numeric card stat. In Guard, the participating Buns combine their values against the Dog's value.
Individual values and other uses remain to be designed. The existing Dog trait also named Cunning is distinct from this new numeric stat; its naming needs review before implementation.
